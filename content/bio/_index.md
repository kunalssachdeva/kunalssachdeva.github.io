### Short bio

Kunal Sachdeva is an Assistant Professor of Finance and the Michael R. and Mary Kay Hallman Faculty Fellow at the Stephen M. Ross School of Business at the University of Michigan. He studies how firms and financial markets respond to environmental regulation and climate risk, with work published in the *Journal of Financial Economics*, the *Review of Financial Studies*, *Management Science*, and *Nature Climate Change*. His research on environmental activist investing received the Moskowitz Prize. He holds a PhD in Finance from Columbia Business School.

### Long bio

Kunal Sachdeva is an Assistant Professor of Finance at the Stephen M. Ross School of Business at the University of Michigan, where he is also the Michael R. and Mary Kay Hallman Fellow. His research sits at the intersection of corporate finance, sustainable investing, and financial intermediation, with a particular focus on environmental, social, and governance issues, innovation, and the real effects of financial markets on firms and society.

His academic work has been published in leading journals, including the *Journal of Financial Economics*, the *Review of Financial Studies*, *Management Science*, and *Nature Climate Change*. His research has received multiple international recognitions, including the Moskowitz Prize for socially responsible investing and several best paper awards at major finance conferences. His work has also been cited in policy and media outlets including the *Financial Times*, *Bloomberg*, and *Reuters*.

Prior to joining the University of Michigan in 2024, he served as an Assistant Professor of Finance at Rice University's Jones Graduate School of Business. He earned his PhD in Finance from Columbia Business School, an M.Fin. from the MIT Sloan School of Management, and dual undergraduate degrees in Economics and Electrical Engineering from Queen's University.

Alongside his research, he has received multiple Teaching Excellence Awards for his instruction in MBA core finance and in electives on sustainable finance and financial markets, and contributes to the profession through editorial service, conference organization, and doctoral advising.

Before entering academia, he worked at BlackRock, conducting research for hedge fund investment strategies, and at Bloomberg LP, as a financial software developer building trading systems.
